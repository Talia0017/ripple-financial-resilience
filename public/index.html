      About the Data & Calculations

      What do these financial figures mean?

      I · Typical monthly money in
      Median money_in across all available months. This includes all money entering the account, not necessarily salary. All displayed amounts are in CAD, following the challenge dataset units.
      S · Typical monthly spending
      Median money_out, including loan payments.
      F · Typical fixed costs
      Add household_bills + insurance + loan_payments within each month, then take the median of those totals. This is not the sum of three separate medians.
      B · Estimated cash buffer
      Mean avg_daily_balance across the last six monthly rows, sorted by month. Use available rows if fewer than six exist and disclose the limitation. This historical estimate is not a live available balance.
      H · Household bills
      Median household_bills. Zero recorded bills do not establish that an account holder has no living expenses.
      P · Loan payment
      Total monthly_payment for loans started by 2025-12-31 with status C/D. A/B and accounts with no matching loans contribute zero. Unavailable loan data remains unknown. P and F are not added to S again.

      The median is the middle value after sorting; with an even number of values, use the average of the two middle values. Missing amounts are not replaced with zero. Relevant statistics use valid records and disclose limitations. B never uses older rows outside its six-row window to replace missing values.

      Official runway formula and model limits

      buffer_left = B − one_off_costs

      gap = spending_after − income_after

      1. If buffer_left ≤ 0 → 0 months

      2. Otherwise, if gap ≤ 0 → No shortfall (120+)

      3. Otherwise → min(buffer_left / gap, 120)

      “No shortfall (120+)” is the official judging label for no monthly shortfall under the current assumptions. It does not guarantee financial security for the next 120 months. A depleted buffer gives a runway of zero, even when income covers monthly spending.

      The model does not verify EI eligibility, payment caps or benefit duration. It does not project taxes, interest, inflation or changes in behaviour, and is not a month-by-month cash-flow forecast. Planning uses separate, illustrative Ripple assumptions; it does not change these official rules.

      Data sources and verification

      The official monthly_summary.csv and loans.csv challenge datasets were provided by Sheridan Datathon 2026 — Tangerine Track. The project owner confirmed with the organizers that they may be publicly used and contain no real customer records.

      Loading dataset information.

A separate compact asset includes each account’s most recent 12 recorded monthly money-in and money-out values. No missing months are invented. The full monthly ledger remains local.

      Scenario assumptions

      • Job loss: monthly money in = I × EI replacement rate (default 55%). This applies to all inflows, not verified salary. Actual EI eligibility, caps and duration are not established.
      • Higher bills: monthly spending = S + H × bill increase (default 15%). Only recorded household bills increase. Zero recorded bills do not mean no living expenses.
      • Emergency repair: subtract the cost once from B. The initial cost equals typical spending S. It is not a recurring expense.

      Ripple planning assumptions

      The example goal is three months of typical baseline spending, separate from official Tangerine formulas. Available buffer is max(scenario buffer, 0). Remaining target is max(goal − available buffer, 0). The example contribution is 50% of positive scenario surplus, limited to the remaining target. Time to goal is rounded up to whole months. No positive surplus means no suggested contribution or date. A met goal needs no further contribution. Missing or non-positive spending makes this example unavailable.

      Timing assumes unchanged cash flow, no interest and no further shocks. A historical balance is not proof of accessible savings, and estimated surplus is not a personal affordability assessment. Backup steps identify calculated gaps and one-time costs; they do not establish reducible expenses, borrowing capacity or benefit eligibility.

      How we checked the dataset findings

      The buffer-to-spending equivalent divides the historical buffer estimate B by typical spending S. It does not subtract incoming money, model future cash flow or calculate official runway. Negative or missing balances are not treated as spendable savings.

      This is a descriptive comparison of challenge accounts, not evidence of cause, a population estimate or a prediction. Different record lengths and timing can affect these summaries. Medians of money in and money out may come from different months.
